The "Chocolate Mango Tango" 🍫 🥭 💃🏻

2-Year Fermented Cayenne, Fresh Chocolate Habanero & The Cacao Epiphany

A slow-blooming table sauce engineered for broad, mouth-coating warmth with zero throat sting. Combines 2-year aged red cayennes, fresh chocolate habanero, and sweet Palmer mango, finished with cocoa powder to unlock rich Mexican mole depth and bring out natural fruit sweetness.

🔥 Step-by-Step Cooking Protocol

  1. Sweat Garlic & Ginger in Oil
    Strain the 2-year fermented cayennes, keeping the brine. Heat 1 tbsp peanut oil in a wide pot over medium-low heat. Sweat the minced garlic and grated ginger with a pinch of salt for 2–3 minutes until soft and fragrant. Do not brown.
    💡 Visual Cue: Garlic should look soft and glossy; keep sizzling very gentle.
  2. Simmer Cayennes, Mango & Vinegar
    Add the strained cayennes, diced chocolate habanero, and diced mango. Pour in 100 ml apple cider vinegar, 100 ml brine, lime juice, and 4 heaping tsp demerara sugar. Bring to a gentle boil, cover partially, and simmer on low for 10–12 minutes until the mango and habanero are completely tender.
    💡 Thermal Target: Heat past 85°C to pasteurise wild yeasts and soften the mango pectin.
  3. Stick-Blend to Sweet-Chilli Consistency
    Submerge an immersion blender to the bottom of the pot. Pulse in short 3-second bursts until you get a glossy, cohesive sauce with visible red chilli flecks. It should coat a spatula and flow like a good store-bought sweet & sour chilli sauce. Do not dilute—it thickens further as it cools.
    🛑 Consistency Check: If droplets melt smoothly back into the sauce without leaving mounds, kill the heat.
  4. Whisk in Maple & Cocoa Slurry
    Stir in 3 tbsp pure dark maple syrup. Scoop 2–3 tbsp of hot sauce into a small cup, whisk in 1 regular tsp of Dutch cocoa powder until smooth, then pour it back into the pot. Simmer on low for 60 seconds while stirring. Taste test on a cooled spoon.
    ✨ The Taste Shift: The bitter cocoa creates a dark contrast floor, cutting the sharp acid and instantly unleashing hidden fruit sweetness.
  5. Jar Hot & Seal
    Ladle hot sauce (>80°C) into clean, warm glass jars, leaving 1 cm headspace. Wipe the rims spotless with a paper towel. Screw metal lids on tight and invert jars for 60 seconds to pasteurise the seal. Flip upright to cool.
    🔊 Auditory Check: Listen for the metallic "pop" as the cooling vacuum pulls the lid button down tight.

🧪 The Food Science (Why It Works)

🛡️ 1. Why It Doesn't Sting (The Lipid Shield)
Capsaicin is fat-soluble. In watery vinegar sauces, free capsaicin hits tongue pain receptors immediately like needle pricks. Sweating aromatics in 1 tbsp oil and stick-blending with mango pectin creates microscopic droplets that coat the tongue, turning a sharp sting into a broad, slow-blooming warmth.
🍫 2. The Cacao Trick (Why Bitter Made It Sweeter)
Bitter alkalized cocoa does two things: it neutralizes sharp acid spikes that numb your sweet taste buds, and it gives your palate a dark contrast floor. Against that dark base, your brain immediately notices the fruit sugars that were already there, making the sauce taste instantly sweeter without added sugar.
🏺 3. Shelf-Stability Guarantee (pH < 3.4)
Lactic acid from the 2-year ferment, acetic acid from vinegar, and citric acid from limes drop the pH well below 3.4 (the safe preservation ceiling is 4.6). Bottled hot into jars with metal plastisol lids, the cooling steam pulls a commercial vacuum seal, keeping unopened jars fresh in the cupboard for over a year.
